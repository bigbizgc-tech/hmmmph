01 · The two paths

One push, or every month.

A boost is one push with a start and an end. A plan keeps the push running month after month. Same real ads, different rhythm.

One-time · No contract

The boost path

Spark · Boost · BlitzFrom $19.993, 5 or 7 days

  1. 01

    Step 1: Claim it

    Send the form or call. Tell us your name, what you’re promoting and the platform you want.

  2. 02

    Step 2: Point it

    Your post, page, channel, site or booking line. Pick where you want new people to land.

  3. 03

    Step 3: Aim it

    At your area or the audience you name. Local shops usually aim by place, creators by the kind of viewer. You OK it before it runs.

  4. 04

    Step 4: It goes live

    We set up a real paid placement through the platform’s own ad tools. Our target is live within 24 hours of payment.

  5. 05

    Step 5: Read the recap

    When the run ends you get the numbers that matter: what was spent, how many people it reached, and the clicks or views it got.

Run ends. Recap in hand, you decide: go again, go bigger, or stop. Nothing renews on its own.

03 · What you get back

The recap, in plain English.

Every boost ends with a recap. Every plan gets one each month. Here is what each number on it means.

SpendEvery recap
What the platform actually charged for the ad. It’s the part of your price that buys the ad itself: about $10 on Spark, $25 on Boost, $50 on Blitz and $150 a month on Surge.
ReachEvery recap
How many different people saw it at least once. Someone who saw it three times counts once.
ImpressionsAd reports
How many times it was shown in total, repeats included. It’s always at least as big as reach.
ClicksEvery recap · link goals
Taps that took someone where you pointed the ad: your page, your link, your site or your booking line.
ViewsEvery recap · video goals
How many times your video was watched. Each platform sets its own rule for what counts as a view, so compare views on the same app.
FollowsBoost, Blitz and plans
People who hit follow while the run was live. Whether they do depends most on what they find when they land on your page.

Read it in this order

Spend first: did the money go out. Then reach: how many people saw it. Then the one number that matches your goal. The rest of the method sits in a classified file further down.

04 · What we never do

What we never do.

Some shortcuts make numbers go up and business go nowhere. We don’t take them.

  • Bots

    No software pretending to be people. Your ad is shown by the platform itself, through its own ad tools.

  • Fake followers

    No padded counts. A follower who doesn’t exist can’t watch, book or buy.

  • Engagement pods

    No groups trading likes and comments to game the feed. It looks busy and brings you nobody new.

  • Buying followers

    Not at any price. New people find you because a real ad put you in front of them.

  • Running without your OK

    You see what runs under your name before it runs. Nothing goes out that you haven’t seen.

  • Promising numbers

    Results depend on your content, your audience and the platform. No set number of views, likes or followers is guaranteed.

Every push is a real paid placement, bought through the platform’s own ad tools, the same ones any business uses.

Classified Clearance 3 · Operative

How a push is actually aimed

This is how we set up every run. Use it on your own ads too.

  1. One goal per run. Pick reach (the most people seeing it), clicks (people going to a link) or video views. The platform chases whatever you ask for, so asking for all three gets you a weak version of each.
  2. Aim by place, then by interest. A shop does best aimed at the area its customers can actually get to, not the whole state. A creator does best aimed at interests close to the content: the games, music or topics the right viewers already follow. Too narrow and the platform can’t find enough people. Too wide and the money spreads thin.
  3. The first two seconds decide everything. People choose to stop or scroll almost instantly. Open on the most interesting frame, not a logo or a slow intro. Put the point on screen in words, because plenty of people watch with the sound off.
  4. One clear call to action. Follow, book, buy or show up. Pick one and say it plainly. An ad that asks for three things usually gets none of them.
  5. Longer runs settle better. A platform spends the first part of any run learning who responds, then puts more of the money in front of people like them. A longer run gives it time to learn. That is why longer runs tend to get more out of each dollar, and why monthly plans don’t restart from zero.
  6. Leave it alone while it learns. Big edits mid-run can send the platform back to learning. Set it up right, then let the run play out before you judge it.
  7. Read the recap like this. Spend first: did the money go out. Reach next: how many people saw it. Then the one number that matches your goal. Compare each run with your last one, not with somebody else’s account. And count what happened off the screen: messages, bookings, people who say they saw you.
